What is DPI?

When using an eDPI calculator, it is compulsory to understand what DPI means. It actually stands for dots per inch, also known as CPI, and it states the pixels your cursor has traveled across the screen when you move the mouse one cm on a table. You can also change this setting with a button or through software from the manufacturer, which adjusts how it feels when holding the mouse while using Windows, playing games, or browsing the web.

Pro gamers usually like the DPI between 400 and 1600 for ideal in-game performance. A higher DPI can cause the sensor to be pushed beyond limits, which causes a smoothing issue, where it predicts movements and ignores raw input. Keeping a balanced sensitivity is key. Finding the perfect ground helps in maintaining precision, consistency, and smoother control during matches.

What is sensitivity?

Sensitivity is an in-game setting that allows players to increase mouse DPI for a specific game and adjust the aim using a reliable method. It's a way of increasing sensitivity rather than simply raising high levels for control and balance. Every gamer knows different games often use their own sensitivity settings, and the same numbers mean two things depending on the title.

Because of this variation, a general setting is needed to compare performance with friends or immediately change to new preferred setups.

What is eDPI, and how to calculate it?

eDPI stands for Effective Dots Per Inch and is calculated by multiplying the Mouse DPI by the In-game Sensitivity
eDPI = DPI × Sensitivity.

It gives an accurate overall measure that determines how fast the cursor moves, helping gamers compare sensitivities across players using different values, hardware, or software. Both settings directly affect aim speed and are multiplied together for accuracy.

How to Use the eDPI Calculator

It is simple to learn how to use the eDPI calculator, which helps you match or improve your aim across games like CS: GO, Valorant, and other FPS games. Follow the steps below to calculate your eDPI value accurately using your DPI and sensitivity settings.

Step Action
Step 1: Check Your Friend's Settings Start by checking your friend's settings.
For example, if their DPI is 1000 and their sensitivity is 0.8, you can calculate the value using the formula:
eDPI = DPI × sensitivity
eDPI = 1000 × 0.8 = 800
This equals 800, meaning your friend's eDPI is 800.
Step 2: Match Your Settings Now check your own settings.
If your DPI is 500, you can find the needed sensitivity by dividing:
sensitivity = eDPI ÷ DPI
sensitivity = 800 ÷ 500 = 1.6

FAQs About eDPI Calculator

What is DPI?

DPI (Dots Per Inch) measures how many pixels your mouse pointer moves on screen per inch of physical mouse movement. Higher DPI means the cursor moves faster, while lower DPI means slower, more precise movement. It's a hardware setting affecting sensitivity but doesn't tell the whole story of in-game sensitivity. Gamers adjust DPI based on personal preference and game requirements.

Can you compare eDPI settings between games?

Yes, eDPI (effective DPI) helps compare mouse sensitivity across different games by multiplying your mouse DPI by the in-game sensitivity. It normalizes differences so you can maintain consistent feel when switching games. However, game mechanics and field of view also affect the actual feel, so eDPI is a guideline rather than exact.

What is the eDPI of 400 DPI and 1.80 mouse sensitivity?

The eDPI is calculated by multiplying DPI and sensitivity: 400 × 1.80 = 720 eDPI. This number represents the effective sensitivity across games and helps to compare or replicate sensitivity settings in other games.

Is mouse acceleration good for gaming?

Mouse acceleration is generally not recommended for competitive gaming because it changes the cursor speed based on how fast you move the mouse, making aiming inconsistent. Most pro players disable acceleration to maintain muscle memory and precision. However, some casual players may prefer it for general use.

Should I choose high or low eDPI?

Choosing high or low eDPI depends on your playstyle and comfort. Low eDPI offers more precision and control, favored by many competitive players, while high eDPI allows faster turning but can reduce accuracy. Experiment to find what suits your aiming style best.

What is DPI and how does it differ from eDPI?

DPI is a hardware measurement of mouse sensor sensitivity, while eDPI (effective DPI) is the product of your mouse DPI and in-game sensitivity. DPI alone doesn't reflect actual cursor speed in-game; eDPI accounts for both hardware and software settings to represent true sensitivity.

What eDPI do professional players use?

Professional players typically use eDPI values ranging from 200 to 800, depending on the game and personal preference. Many FPS pros prefer lower eDPI for better precision, often between 400 and 600. However, there is no one-size-fits-all as comfort and playstyle vary.

How do I find my optical eDPI?

To find your optical eDPI, multiply your mouse's DPI setting by the in-game sensitivity you use. If your mouse software shows your DPI, just multiply that by your current game sensitivity setting to get the effective eDPI value.

What does 360 degree turn mean?

A 360 degree turn means moving your mouse just enough to rotate your in-game character or camera fully around once. It's often used as a measure of sensitivity, indicating how much physical mouse movement is required to spin completely around.
